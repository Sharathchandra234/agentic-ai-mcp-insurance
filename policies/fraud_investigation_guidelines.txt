FRAUD INVESTIGATION AND RISK ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES

Document ID:
FRAUD-GUIDE-001

Version:
2026.1

1. PURPOSE

These guidelines describe how fraud risk indicators may be considered during insurance claim investigation.

2. RISK INDICATORS

Potential risk indicators include:

- High claim frequency.
- Repeated claims involving similar circumstances.
- Unusual claim amounts.
- Repair-cost inconsistencies.
- Vehicle information inconsistencies.
- Customer information inconsistencies.
- Incident descriptions that conflict with available evidence.
- Suspicious or incomplete documentation.

3. MACHINE LEARNING RISK SCORES

Machine-learning systems can estimate the probability of a claim being associated with patterns observed in training data.

A fraud probability should be interpreted as a risk indicator rather than proof of fraud.

4. RISK LEVELS

For demonstration purposes, an internal risk system may categorize model output as:

Low:
Fraud probability below 40%.

Medium:
Fraud probability from 40% to below 70%.

High:
Fraud probability of 70% or above.

These thresholds are demonstration thresholds and should not be interpreted as regulatory standards.

5. MODEL LIMITATIONS

Model performance depends on:

- Training data quality.
- Feature quality.
- Data distribution.
- Model design.
- Changes in claim patterns.

A model trained on synthetic or demonstration data should not be assumed to represent real-world insurer fraud behavior.

6. HUMAN REVIEW

Claims with elevated risk indicators should receive appropriate human review.

Investigators should examine the evidence before reaching a conclusion.

7. FINAL DECISION

An automated fraud-risk score must not independently determine whether a claim is fraudulent.

Final decisions should follow applicable policy terms, investigation procedures, evidence, and authorized human review.
