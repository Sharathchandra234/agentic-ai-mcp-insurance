P&C CLAIMS INVESTIGATION GUIDELINES

Document ID:
CLAIMS-GUIDE-001

Version:
2026.1

1. PURPOSE

These guidelines provide a general framework for investigating motor insurance claims.

They should be used together with the applicable insurance policy and claim-specific evidence.

2. INITIAL VERIFICATION

The investigator should verify:

- Claim ID.
- Policy number.
- Policy status.
- Policy coverage.
- Customer identity.
- Vehicle identity.
- Incident date.
- Reported claim amount.

3. POLICY VERIFICATION

Before assessing coverage, confirm:

- The policy was active on the relevant incident date.
- The reported event falls within the policy coverage.
- Applicable deductibles and limits.
- Relevant exclusions.

4. CUSTOMER VERIFICATION

Review available customer information, including:

- Customer history.
- Previous claims.
- Customer relationship with the insurer.
- Relevant claim patterns.

Previous claims should be treated as contextual information and not as proof of fraudulent behavior.

5. VEHICLE VERIFICATION

Verify:

- Vehicle registration.
- Vehicle make and model.
- Vehicle year.
- Vehicle ownership.
- Reported vehicle condition.
- Repair estimates.

6. DAMAGE VERIFICATION

Compare:

- Reported incident circumstances.
- Photographs.
- Inspection findings.
- Repair estimates.
- Repair invoices.

Material discrepancies should be investigated.

7. FRAUD RISK INDICATORS

Potential indicators may include:

- Unusual claim frequency.
- Large claim amount relative to available evidence.
- Significant repair-cost discrepancies.
- Repeated similar claims.
- Contradictory incident descriptions.
- Suspicious documentation.

These indicators should trigger further investigation rather than an automatic fraud determination.

8. AI AND ML ASSISTANCE

Machine-learning models may be used to prioritize claims for additional review.

A model prediction represents a risk signal and should not be treated as definitive proof of fraud.

Model outputs should be reviewed together with policy terms, claim evidence, and investigation findings.

9. HUMAN REVIEW

Final claim decisions should be made by authorized claims professionals.

AI systems should assist investigation and decision support rather than independently approving or rejecting claims.

10. INVESTIGATION SUMMARY

A final investigation summary should clearly separate:

- Facts obtained from records.
- Applicable policy provisions.
- Model-generated risk indicators.
- Investigator observations.
- Recommended next steps.
